READABILITY AND INCLUSIVE ACCESS POLICY


1.0 Background

1.1 The sovereign digital estate at nicksonogwal.org exists to reverse epistemicide — the historical destruction of knowledge, memory, and cultural identity. This reversal is not symbolic; it is structural. It demands that the custodians of memory, especially elders and visually challenged persons, encounter content that is readable, comfortable, and dignified.

1.2 Readable design is not decoration — it is architecture. It determines who can access my knowledge, who can cite me, and who can participate in the reversal of epistemicide. A digital estate that cannot be read by elders is not sovereign, not inclusive, and not future‑proof.

1.3 Readable content is survival. If elders cannot read my work, then the knowledge I am preserving and restoring becomes inaccessible to the very custodians of memory. This policy therefore protects the ancestral chain of transmission — ensuring that wisdom flows from generation to generation without obstruction.

1.4 Readable design is a political act. It ensures that my digital estate is not biased toward youth, speed, or Western design norms. It keeps my work grounded in community, heritage, and intergenerational continuity. It affirms that knowledge must be accessible to those who carry the deepest memory of our people.

1.5 This policy establishes mandatory readability standards for all publications, pages, and documents within the sovereign digital estate. It ensures that content is accessible, dignified, and usable across generations, and that the architecture of the site remains aligned with Story. Memory. Sovereignty.

1.6 The purpose of this policy is to guarantee that all content within the sovereign digital estate remains accessible, dignified, and readable for all generations, ensuring continuity of knowledge and cultural memory.


2.0 Rationale

2.1 The rationale for this policy is ancestral, cultural, legal, and architectural. It arises from the duty to protect elders as custodians of memory, from the obligation to preserve cultural knowledge, and from the sovereign responsibility to design digital spaces that honour the dignity of all readers.

2.2 In Lango cosmology, elders are the living archives of the community. They carry the oral histories, genealogies, rituals, and cosmological truths that anchor identity. A digital estate that excludes them becomes complicit in epistemicide. This policy therefore affirms the ancestral principle: Woro acaki Kwo — respect for origin.

2.3 Ugandan law reinforces this ancestral duty. Section 21(1) of the Persons with Disabilities Act, 2020 states: “A person with a disability has the right to access information in a form that is accessible and usable.” This policy operationalizes that legal obligation within the sovereign digital estate.

2.4 The rationale is also architectural. A sovereign digital estate must be readable across generations, devices, and contexts. It must resist the global trend of designing for speed over substance, youth over elders, and aesthetics over accessibility. This policy ensures that the architecture remains human‑centred and culturally grounded.

2.5 Together, these rationales form the foundation for the policy statement that follows.


3.0 Policy Statement

3.1 All content published on nicksonogwal.org shall be readable, accessible, and inclusive for older persons, visually challenged readers, and multi‑generational audiences.

3.2 Readable design is a non‑negotiable architectural requirement. It is integral to the sovereign identity of the digital estate and shall be applied consistently across all content types.

3.3 This policy applies to longform essays, policy documents, archival materials, metadata blocks, static pages, and all future publications.

3.4 This policy applies to all contributors, editors, custodians, and automated systems responsible for generating, publishing, or maintaining content on the sovereign digital estate.

4.0 Readability Standards

4.1 Body text shall be set between eighteen and twenty pixels to ensure comfortable reading for older persons. Headings shall scale proportionally between twenty‑four and thirty‑two pixels.

4.2 Line height shall be set between one point six and one point eight to prevent crowding and support effortless scanning.

4.3 Line length shall be maintained between sixty and seventy‑five characters to preserve rhythm and reduce eye strain.

4.4 Text contrast shall be high, using charcoal on soft white. Pure black on pure white is prohibited due to glare.

4.5 All content shall be tested for readability on mobile, tablet, and desktop devices at elder reading distance.


5.0 Design Enhancements Supporting Readability

5.1 Adaptive text sizing shall be provided through an A, A+, and A++ toggle implemented using CSS variables.

5.2 Humanist serif typefaces shall be used for longform reading, and geometric sans typefaces shall be used for user interface elements.

5.3 Generous spacing shall be applied around paragraphs and sections to support visual comfort.

5.4 Visual noise shall be minimized. Thin fonts, low‑contrast grey text, and unnecessary decorative elements shall not be used.

5.5 Accessible color themes, including high‑contrast and warm (sepia) modes, shall be provided for readers with light sensitivity.

6.0 Implementation Framework

6.1 The Digital Sovereignty Governance Desk shall oversee implementation, compliance, and continuous improvement of this policy.

6.2 All new content shall undergo a Readability Compliance Check before publication. This check shall verify type size, spacing, contrast, line length, and accessibility modes.

6.3 A quarterly Readability Audit shall be conducted. The audit shall review a representative sample of pages, test readability for older readers, evaluate accessibility modes, and recommend improvements.

6.4 Audit results shall be documented, archived, and used to inform policy updates.

7.0 Continuous Improvement

7.1 This policy shall evolve based on user feedback, accessibility research, technological advancements, and cultural considerations.

7.2 Updates shall be versioned and published with changelogs to preserve transparency and provenance.

8.0 Application and Use

8.1 This policy shall guide all content creation, inform design decisions, support accessibility compliance, strengthen sovereign digital identity, and ensure intergenerational usability.

8.2 It shall be used as both a design instrument and a governance tool across the sovereign digital estate.


9.0 Immediate Actions

9.1 Increase body text to eighteen pixels.
9.2 Set line height to one point seven.
9.3 Add text size toggles.
9.4 Switch to humanist serif fonts for longform pages.
9.5 Add high‑contrast and warm modes.
9.6 Widen margins and spacing.
9.7 Test pages at elder reading distance on tablet and desktop.
